## Why these choices

**Title.**
- I checked the 25 most-viewed vending videos of the last four years. Our own study of 649 outlier videos found that a real dollar figure in the title makes a video 1.9× more likely to break out. A question mark cuts that to 0.64×, and the word "boring" to 0.5×.
- vidIQ's 2026 study of over 4 million videos also found question titles do worse (0.82×).
- YouTube's own advice is to keep titles short with the key words first. So all three titles are 46–59 characters with "vending machine" in the first 35.
- The main title is a fact, then a twist ($900 in, $197 kept). That opens a curiosity gap the video pays off with real documents, and YouTube now penalises broken promises.
- The three test titles make three different promises: the owner's cut, the buyer's payback, and the viewer's own $2. Test & Compare needs that difference to pick a winner.
- No "passive income", "SHOCKED", all caps or "?".
- A new rival matters here: Every Level HQ's "Reality vs. Internet Math" vending video drew 161K views in its first week. The sourced-numbers angle isn't empty any more, so the documents on screen are what set us apart.

**Description.**
- The first two lines are what shows before "…more" (about 100–160 characters on desktop), so they carry the hook.
- They use the two phrases people actually type, which I took from Google and YouTube autocomplete:
  - "how much does a vending machine make"
  - "is a vending machine business worth it"
- The chapters are named after the voiceover. Google shows them as "key moments" in search.
- Each source gets its own link, which backs up the "sourced, no hype" promise.
- YouTube says hashtags beyond three don't show by the title, so there are three.

**Tags.**
- YouTube says tags play a minimal role, mostly catching misspellings. So these are the searched phrases plus one common misspelling.
- "Passive income" and "side hustle" stay in the tags because people search them. They're kept out of the title, since Reddit threads mock vending as "the opposite of passive".
