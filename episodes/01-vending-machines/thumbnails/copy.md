# Thumbnail copy: the line that makes someone click

The title asks the question ("How Much Profit Vending Machines Really Make"). The thumbnail has to make the viewer *need* the answer. The best way to do that is to show the number they already know and hide the one they don't.

## Pick: **$900 IN → $??? KEPT**

On a real, glowing vending machine:
- "$900 a month" in white.
- An arrow to a yellow box that says "$???".

**Why someone watches**
- **It's the question our viewer already has.** They've seen the sales pitch. They're 40–65 and have the money to buy a route. "What would I actually keep?" is the reason they'd watch at all.
- **It shows the known half and hides the surprising half.** That's the curiosity gap strategists rank first. It's also what the biggest vending thumbnails ever did: Quik Play's top three hits (10.6M, 2.5M, 2.0M views) all hide the number ("HOW MUCH?!").
- **It's honest, and the video pays it off.** The supplier's own model of a machine selling $900 a month gives about $197 kept, before the owner pays themselves (fact-checked, script line 52).
- **It doesn't repeat the title.** The title says "profit"; the thumbnail adds the $900 and hides the rest.

## Other lines, if you prefer

- **"14 YEARS?"** on a machine. This is the fear line for buyers: a $3,500 machine can take from about 20 months to more than 14 years to pay for itself. It's a shock, and it doesn't explain itself. The weakness is that the video only gets there around 8:30.
- **"IT'S NOT THE OWNER"** with an arrow at the machine. This is the mystery line: someone else gets most of your $2 (the drink company, the building, the card companies). It's intriguing but vaguer.

## Why the earlier ones didn't work

- **62¢, M and G** gave the answer away.
- **"FOLLOW THE $2"** only described the video. It didn't raise a question the viewer cares about.
