# Ep 2 retention review (script-v2)

Reviewed Nov 27, 2026. Spoken words now: **1,998** (headings and `[VISUAL]` lines excluded). Net effect of all edits below: **about −140, to about 1,858**.
No new facts or numbers: every edit reorders, cuts or rephrases text already in the script.

## What works
- **The hook lands:** "$30 a month" is the first word. By about 15 seconds the viewer has the list of who gets paid before the owner.
- **The Split chapter** is the strongest stretch: one slice per line, a running tally, and the title figure as the payoff.
- **The arc holds up:** churn → "So who wins?" → land / brand / PE → too many washes → buying one → checklist.
- **The Zips → Getty link** works as a callback: the "safe" landlord gets hurt too.

## Problems
- **The title figure ($6.30) doesn't come up until about 3 minutes in.** The hook never confirms the title.
- **The main open loop is muddled.** "The biggest winners aren't the ones you'd expect" is followed straight away by an example of a *loser* (the $15 → $7 drop). Then the close ("The winners … tend to own the land …") never says it is the answer.
- **Lecture risk** comes in three places: the methods paragraph at the start of the Split, the list of equipment owners, and the run of three late add-ons (comp sales, the SBA 504 aside, water).
- **Repetition:** seven paragraphs start with "So".

## Edits, in priority order

### 1. Hook: confirm the title figure in the first 20 seconds (+10 words)
- **Current:** "Before the owner of the tunnel keeps a cent, the workers, the landlord, the utilities, the chemical supplier and the brand all get paid."
- **Replace with:** "Before the owner of the tunnel keeps a cent, the workers, the landlord, the utilities, the chemical supplier and the brand all get paid. By our estimate, the owner is left with about 6 dollars 30."
- **Why:** this matches the title on screen and promises the breakdown that follows. The "Net to Owner estimate" source line is already in the visual.

### 2. Hook: make the open loop match the example that follows it (+1)
- **Current:** "And the biggest winners aren't the ones you'd expect. In 2021, that chain, Mister Car Wash, …"
- **Replace with:** "And the big investors you'd expect to win haven't always. In 2021, that chain, Mister Car Wash, …"

### 3. Plant the loop clearly at the end of the cold open (+5)
- **Current:** "Here's where the thirty dollars goes, and who ends up holding the bill."
- **Replace with:** "Here's where the thirty dollars goes, who ends up holding the bill, and who really comes out ahead."

### 4. Pay off the loop by name in the close (+5)

### 5. Use the exact title figure in the recap (+1)
- **Current:** "And the owner keeps about six dollars, if the members stay and no new tunnel opens down the road."
- **Replace with:** "And the owner keeps about 6 dollars 30, if the members stay and no new tunnel opens down the road."

### 6. Shorten the methods paragraph, the first dead stretch (−18)
- **Current:** "So let's follow the thirty dollars. We built this from three sources. The costs are what Tommy's Express franchise sites open five years or more reported in the company's 2025 franchise disclosure. Those sites averaged about 2.2 million dollars in sales in 2024. The rent is our estimate, from the average asking rent on car washes listed for sale in September 2026. The money set aside for equipment comes from Mister Car Wash's annual report. Some states, like Wisconsin, add sales tax on top. We'll leave tax out."
- **Replace with:** "Now, let's follow the thirty dollars. The costs come from Tommy's Express franchise sites open five years or more, as reported in its 2025 franchise disclosure. Those sites averaged about 2.2 million dollars in sales in 2024. The rent is our estimate, from average asking rents on car washes for sale in September 2026. The equipment reserve comes from Mister Car Wash's annual report. And we've left out sales tax."
- **Why:** it gets to the first slice about 7 seconds sooner. Using "Now" instead of "So" also cuts one of the seven "So" openers.

A conveyor pulls your car through a long building, and the vacuums outside are usually free."
