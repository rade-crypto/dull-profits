## 2. Principles that hold up

1. **Real, specific numbers.** In our niche a $ figure gives ×1.9 ([decision report](/home/user/dull-profits/docs/decision-report.md)). MrBeast's guide: "50 Hours In My Front Yard" loses to "…In Ketchup" ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)). Caveat: across all of YouTube, titles with numbers get ~11% fewer views ([1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/what-actually-makes-a-youtube-video-go-viral-in-2025)), so the number must be the story itself.
2. **Statements beat questions.** A question mark scored ×0.64 in our niche, and question-ending titles scored 0.82× across 4M+ videos ([vidIQ research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-title-formulas-study/)).
3. **A curiosity gap made from a twist.** Paddy Galloway: open a curiosity gap; be "familiar but unexpected" ([Colin & Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)). In vidIQ's study, "Inside X" scored 2.5×, "do not / stop / never" nearly 2× and "X years" nearly 1.5× ([vidIQ research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-title-formulas-study/)).
4. **Short, with the key words first.** YouTube says to "keep it short and put the most important words near the beginning" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)); vidIQ says the same for the search phrase ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-video-title/)). Titles of 30 characters or fewer get ~60% more views, and those near 70 do worst ([1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/what-actually-makes-a-youtube-video-go-viral-in-2025)). TV now leads US watch time ([IBC](https://www.ibc.org/ott-streaming/news/tv-set-is-primary-device-for-youtube-viewing-says-ceo-neal-mohan/21662)). I found no official TV cutoff, so I assume the first ~40 characters carry the hook. Aim for 45–60.
5. **The title and thumbnail split the job.** "Don't repeat the title in the thumbnail - ever!", and use three words or fewer ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnails-5-expert-tips/)). Thumbnail text under 10 characters works best ([1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/what-actually-makes-a-youtube-video-go-viral-in-2025)). YouTube shows the two "working together to create interest and tell a story" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)).
6. **Honest curiosity.** Inaccurate titles make viewers stop watching, "which can impact discoverability" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)). Titles that don't "deliver what was promised" break policy ([policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)), and YouTube has enforced against "egregious clickbait" since December 2024 ([Google](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/strengthening-enforcement-against-egregious-clickbait-on-youtube/)). "Curiosity is fine. A broken promise is not" ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-video-title/)).
7. **Test & Compare.** Up to 3 titles; the most watch time wins, not the best CTR. It is set up in desktop Studio, finishes within two weeks, and needs meaningfully different options ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)). Viewers see the same version on every device ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-title-a-b-testing-rolls-out-globally-to-creators/562571/)).
