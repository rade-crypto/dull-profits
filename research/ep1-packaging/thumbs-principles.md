# Thumbnails and Test & Compare: research for Episode 1

Researched Nov 27, 2026. Most studies cited are observational: they show what winners share, not what causes clicks.

## 1. What Test & Compare does today

- **Modes:** "Title only", "Thumbnail only" or "Title and thumbnail", with up to 3 options per test [1]. Title testing reached all channels with advanced features worldwide on Dec 4, 2025 [3].
- **Winner metric:** watch time share, not CTR. YouTube says this rewards accurate expectations [1][3]. Studio shows no CTR for a test [26].
- **Outcomes:** *Winner* (statistically significant), *Performed same* (pick either), or *Inconclusive* (no strong difference, so the **first** option you uploaded stays the default) [1].
- **Duration:** from a few days up to 2 weeks. It ends early once one option is significantly ahead [1][2]. YouTube gives no minimum impressions; more views make a winner more likely [1][25].
- **Assignment:** options run at the same time on equal audience shares [1][2]. Each viewer "will only ever see the same thumbnail variant on the same account," so a signed-in viewer sees one variant on every device, except after switching accounts or via links shared off YouTube [2]. A small control group sees only the default and is left out of the results [1].
- **Eligibility:** desktop Studio only, and advanced features are required. Shorts, made-for-kids, private videos and scheduled Premieres are excluded; no subscriber minimum is stated [1]. A new channel gets advanced features through ID or video verification (not offered to everyone) or later through channel history; phone verification unlocks only intermediate features [4].
- **Rules:** editing the title or thumbnail during a test stops it [1]. If any thumbnail is under 1280x720, all of them are downscaled to 480p [1]. Similar options take longer to resolve, and YouTube suggests testing older videos first [1].

## 2. Thumbnail principles, by strength of evidence

1. **Few words or none.** In 1of10's study of over 300,000 videos, thumbnails with text got about 19% fewer views. The best performers had no text, or under 10 characters covering less than 7% of the frame [7]. In vidIQ's study of 500 breakout videos, 28% had no text, and those with text used a median of 5 words [8]. Eden saw 4–6 words slightly favored [9]. Common ground: one short phrase.
2. **Complement the title, don't repeat it.** Of readable thumbnail text on 1M+ view videos, only 4.1% repeated the title exactly; 74.7% added new wording [18]. vidIQ: "complement your title, not repeat it" [17]. Colin & Samir's Paddy Galloway episode credits a "clear curiosity gap" for a breakout [19].
3. **Faces are optional.** In 1of10's data, thumbnails with and without faces performed about the same. Faces mainly helped channels with 200K+ subscribers, and business videos did worse with faces [7][10]. In Eden's sample, 84.2% of breakouts had a face versus 82.2% of underperformers: "the norm, not an edge" [9]. YouTube says casual viewers respond to "universally relatable actions and emotions" [11]. (vidIQ found more faces in bigger breakouts [8].)
4. **Put a bright subject on a dark ground.** vidIQ: contrast "is what survives the shrink"; avoid YouTube's own red, white and black [8]. 1of10: "Dark thumbnails consistently underperform" [7]. Eden found no brightness effect [9]. For a navy brand: keep the focal element bright (white number, yellow highlighter).
5. **Design it as a TV poster.** TV "is now the primary device for YouTube viewing in the U.S. (by watch time)" [12]. YouTube says to treat thumbnails "as movie posters" at 1280x720 or larger, and to keep them "clean, balanced and easy to read" [13]. 4K thumbnails (up to 50MB) are allowed [14]. TV viewers sit about 3 m away, so limit text [15].
6. **One idea, one focal point.** YouTube: "Try not to make the design too complex," and make the image as large as possible because thumbnails appear at different sizes [11]. MrBeast's thumbnail lead says to make it "easy to understand" and to check it at small size [21].
7. **Older viewers need large, high-contrast type.** In NN/g's research with 123 users aged 65+, tiny or light text, poor contrast and clutter were persistent problems; eyesight declines from the 40s [16].

- **Numbers:** no thumbnail study found. In 1of10's data, titles with numbers got about 11% fewer views [7]. The number is this video's story, so test it.
- **Title length:** 1of10 found titles of 30 characters or fewer got about 60% more views [7]; Eden found the opposite [9]. The main title is 59 characters; YouTube advises key words first [11].

## 3. Designing a test on a new channel

- **Test concepts, not tweaks.** YouTube asks for "meaningfully different" options [1].
- **Change one variable per test.** A "Title and thumbnail" test reports which pair won, not which half earned it [1]. Keep the thumbnail fixed in a title test, and the title fixed in a thumbnail test.
- **Run tests in sequence.** Studio runs one mode per test, and edits stop it [1]. Test the thumbnail first; TV shows it poster-size [13].
- **Impressions:** half of channels and videos have a CTR of 2–10%, and new channels vary more [5]. By the standard sample-size formula [6] (my calculation), telling a 4% CTR from a 5% CTR with 80% power takes about 6,700 impressions per option. Watch time share is noisier, so a real test likely needs more. On a small channel, two options work better than three.
- **Inconclusive:** no strong difference, so the first option stays [1]. A visible 60/40 split is not proof [25]. Don't rerun near-identical options; note it and try bolder concepts later [25].

## 4. Pitfalls

- A thumbnail that "misleads viewers to think they're about to view something that's not in the video" is prohibited and can lead to a strike [22]. "Malicious clickbait" falls under the spam policy [23]. YouTube removes "egregious clickbait" (promises the video doesn't deliver), starting in India with news [24].
- Misleading titles make viewers leave and hurt discoverability [11]. MrBeast's guide (reportedly leaked) says the title and thumbnail "set the expectations," and viewers who feel misled "click off" [20].
- Every option must make the promise the video keeps: $900 in sales, $197 to the owner.
