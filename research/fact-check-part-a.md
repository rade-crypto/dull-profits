## Claim 1: US vending operator revenue

**Claim:** "In 2025, US vending operators brought in more than seven billion dollars."

**Verdict: VERIFIED.** Attribute it to IBISWorld on screen or in narration.

**Source:** IBISWorld, "Vending Machine Operators in the US", industry report 1113 (US NAICS 45421 / 454210). Pages:
- https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/vending-machine-operators/1113/ (current edition; page says "The most recent publication was released August 2026")
- https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/vending-machine-operators/1113/

**Excerpts:**
- Current (Aug 2026) edition: "The market size of the Vending Machine Operators industry in the United States is $8.0bn in 2026." "The market size of the Vending Machine Operators in the US decreased -0.5% in 2026." "has been declining at a CAGR of 1.5 % between 2021 and 2026." Taken together, these put 2025 at about $8.0bn.
- Earlier (September 2025) edition, seen only in search-result snippets of an earlier page version, not fetched directly: revenue of "$7.7bn" for 2025, down 1.3% in 2025. A March 2026 version reportedly said $7.9bn.
- Industry definition: "Vending machine operators provide and service automated machines that sell merchandise – primarily snack foods and soft drinks, but also cigarettes, ice cream, newspapers and other goods." "This industry does not include revenue from soft drink producers that operate their own vending machines."

**Notes:**
- Every IBISWorld edition I found puts 2025 between $7.7bn and $8.0bn, so "more than seven billion" holds whichever one a viewer finds. IBISWorld revises its history between editions, and the full report is paywalled.
- Expect viewers to cite much bigger figures. Those measure the wider "convenience services" industry (vending plus micro markets, office coffee and pantry service), not vending machines alone:
  - NAMA Foundation / Technomic census: "Convenience services industry revenue is expected to reach $31.1 billion in 2025" (VendingMarketWatch, Mar 20, 2026, https://www.vendingmarketwatch.com/management/news/55365563/...; census at namanow.org/foundation/census/).
  - Automatic Merchandiser State of the Industry 2026: "$40.04 billion" for 2025 (Jul 16, 2026, https://www.vendingmarketwatch.com/reports/article/55381943/...).
- Cantaloupe's own network recorded $3.3B in vending sales in 2025 (claim 4h), which makes an industry total of about $8B plausible.

**Suggested line (optional, adds attribution):** "In 2025, US vending machine operators brought in more than seven billion dollars, according to the research firm IBISWorld."

## Claim 2: Market concentration

**Claim:** "No single company controls more than 5 percent of that. Even giants like Compass Group and Aramark are just bigger dots on a map full of small, local businesses."

**Verdict: NEEDS FIX (the 5% figure). The rest is VERIFIED.**

**Why the 5% figure fails:**
- IBISWorld's US report page describes its Companies chapter this way: "This includes data and analysis on companies operating in the industry that hold a market share greater than 5%." It then says: "Companies covered in the Vending Machine Operators industry in the United States include Compass Group Plc and Aramark Corp." So by IBISWorld's own description, both companies hold **more than** 5%. That is the opposite of the script. (https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/vending-machine-operators/1113/, Aug 2026 edition.)
- IBISWorld also says "Compass Group Plc ... holds the most market share" and "The level of competition is high and steady." An earlier edition said the industry "has low market share concentration and the largest business is Compass Group Plc."
- Older IBISWorld figures, quoted second-hand by a supplier blog (eVending, dated Aug 2, 2023, but framed "As it stands in 2016"): "Compass Group PLC (12.5%), and Aramark Corporation (9.0%)". These are dated and not primary. Use them only as a sign that Compass has long been above 5%.
- Current exact shares are paywalled, so I found no public source for any specific current percentage. Do not state a number.

**Aramark as an example: fair.** IBISWorld names Aramark Corp as one of the two biggest companies. Aramark Refreshments runs vending and micro markets in the US and is actively buying regional operators (see claim 7): Tomdra and SunDun (July 15, 2024) and Legend Food Service (Feb 24, 2026).

**Suggested rewrite (L15):**
> "In 2025, US vending machine operators brought in more than seven billion dollars, according to the research firm IBISWorld. The biggest names are food service giants Compass Group and Aramark. But most of the map is still covered in small, local businesses."

## Claim 4: Cantaloupe network statistics

**Primary source for every figure below:** Cantaloupe Inc. ("A 365 Retail Markets company"), *Micropayment Trends Report 2026*, PDF, 17 pp. https://www.cantaloupe.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Micropayment-Trends-Report-2026-Final.pdf (landing page: https://www.cantaloupe.com/resource-center/micropayment-trends-report-2026/). Period covered: "January - December 2025, US & Canada." Data sources: "Cantaloupe transaction data" plus "Mastercard SpendingPulse". Published around mid-2026 (exact date not found).

**Year labels:** every figure in the script that cites this report is a 2025 figure, which is correct. Only "last year" (L45, L61) needs changing to "in 2025", so the line stays right after the video is published.

### 4a. Card $2.45 vs cash $1.57: VERIFIED
p. 9: "Cashless accounted for 78% of all vending sales, up from 73% in 2024, and continues to drive higher spend per transaction — averaging $2.45 versus $1.57 in cash (+59%)".
Wording note: the report's "cashless" covers "credit or debit cards, mobile wallets, loyalty programs, or campus cards" (p. 15). "Card customers" is a slight simplification. "People paying by card or phone" is more accurate.

### 4b. 78% cashless: VERIFIED (2025)
p. 4: "At vending machines, cashless payments reached 78% of total sales, up from 73% the year prior."

### 4c. 600,000+ connected machines and kiosks, US and Canada, 2025: VERIFIED
p. 1: "621K Connected devices studied". Methodology (p. 15) breaks this down as 581,202 food and beverage vending machines, 31,938 amusement and gaming devices, 7,451 micro market locations and 572 Smart Store locations. "More than 600,000" is accurate. The total includes about 32,000 arcade and amusement machines.

### 4d. "about four and a half billion dollars in sales": NEEDS FIX (minor)
p. 1: "$4.6B Total self-service spend, 2025". The components ($3.3B vending, $1.13B micro markets, $178M amusement, $6.3M smart stores) add up to about $4.61B.
Fix: "about 4.6 billion dollars" or "more than four and a half billion dollars".
Do not confuse this with Cantaloupe's FY2025 10-K figure: "total dollar volume of transactions" of $3,444 million for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2025. That is a different measure over a different period.

### 4e. Average purchase $2.01 / $3.02 / $4.49: VERIFIED
p. 6: "In 2025, the average vending transaction was $2.01. Micro markets averaged $3.02 — while Smart Stores reached $4.49, the highest of any self-service format." The landing page repeats this.
**Inconsistency in the source:** the micro market detail page (p. 10) shows a different "$3.42 Avg. ticket*, ↑ 28% YoY", footnoted "*Inclusive of entire micro market data set". p. 5 says the micro market ticket rose from $2.84 to $3.02 (+6.3%). The $3.02 headline figure is the one Cantaloupe repeats on its website, so it is safe to use. Just don't also quote "+28%".
**Do not mix editions:** the 2025 edition (2024 data) gave a 2024 vending average of $2.11 and cashless vs cash of $2.24 vs $1.78. The 2026 edition restates 2024 vending as $1.99 because the data set changed ("Year over year comparison data has been adjusted", p. 15).

### 4f. Micro markets >$1B, up 13%: VERIFIED (2025)
p. 10: "Micro markets crossed the $1 billion mark in sales for the second consecutive year — reaching $1.13 billion in 2025, an increase of 13%". Locations: 7,451, up 19.7%.

### 4g. Smart stores 71 to 572: VERIFIED (2024 to 2025)
p. 11: "+705% location growth — from 71 to 572." Context: smart store total sales were "$6.3M" in 2025, tiny next to $3.3B for vending. Mentioning this keeps the growth claim from overselling the format.

### 4h. Vending ~$3.3B on the network, 2025: VERIFIED
p. 9: "Consumers spent over $3.3 billion at vending machines in 2025 — a 5% increase year over year."

**Usage caveat:** the report's p. 15 disclaimer says "This material may not be published or distributed." Quoting individual statistics with attribution is normal practice. Avoid showing the PDF pages on screen.

**Suggested rewrites:**
- L45: "Card payments do help the operator in one way. In 2025, people paying by card or phone spent an average of 2.45 dollars per purchase at vending machines on Cantaloupe's payment network, versus 1.57 in cash. Card fees cost a few cents, but card customers spend more. In 2025, 78 percent of vending sales on that network were cashless."
- L55: "Cantaloupe, one of the biggest, tracked more than 600,000 connected machines and kiosks across the US and Canada in 2025. Together they handled about 4.6 billion dollars in sales."
- L61: "Micro markets on that network sold more than a billion dollars' worth of goods in 2025, up 13 percent. Smart stores grew from 71 locations to 572 in a single year, though together they still sold only about 6 million dollars."

## Claim 5: Cantaloupe ownership and revenue model

**Verdict: the description is VERIFIED but incomplete. The ownership change has happened, and the script should mention it.**

**Deal timeline (primary sources):**
- **Announced June 16, 2025.** Cantaloupe/365 joint press release, filed with the SEC as DEFA14A Ex. 99.1, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896429/000110465925059759/tm2518071d2_ex99-1.htm: "Cantaloupe shareholders will receive $11.20 per share in cash"; "an all-cash transaction with an equity value of approximately $848 million"; "a 34% premium to Cantaloupe's unaffected closing stock price on May 30, 2025"; "expected to close in the second half of 2025". (Per the search summary of the 10-K, shareholders approved the deal on Sept 4, 2025.)
- **Closing delayed by FTC review.** FTC press release, May 1, 2026, "FTC Takes Action to Protect Consumers from Anticompetitive Effects of Micromarket Kiosks Deal", https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-takes-action-protect-consumers-anticompetitive-effects-micromarket-kiosks-deal. The deal "would combine the two largest providers of micromarket kiosks as well as related software and services." "365 Retail will be required to divest Cantaloupe's Three Square Market business" to Seaga Manufacturing. The release also imposes interoperability terms and a 10-year prior-notice requirement on future micromarket-kiosk acquisitions. Final order approved 2–0 on June 17, 2026: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-approves-final-consent-order-micromarket-kiosks-deal
- **Closed May 11, 2026.** 365 Retail Markets press release (Business Wire), "365 Retail Markets Completes Acquisition of Cantaloupe to Expand Technology Capabilities and Customer Value", https://365retailmarkets.com/blog/365-retail-markets-completes-acquisition-cantaloupe-expand-technology-capabilities-and (also cantaloupeinc.gcs-web.com/node/22761/pdf). It says 365 "today announced that it has completed its acquisition of Cantaloupe, Inc." "The combined private company..." and "365 is a portfolio company of Providence Equity Partners". The closing release gives no price. The $848M figure comes from the 2025 announcement.

**Revenue model:** Cantaloupe Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896429/000162828025041775/ctlp-20250630.htm. Total revenue was $302.5M: transaction fees $179.5M (59%), subscription fees $83.6M (28%), equipment sales $39.4M (13%). It says: "The majority of customers pay a monthly service fee plus a blended percentage rate on transaction volumes." Active customers were 34,896 and active devices 1.28 million (as of June 30, 2025, worldwide).
So "earns a slice of each card sale" is accurate for its largest revenue line. More than a quarter of revenue is monthly subscription fees, and some comes from selling hardware.

**Suggested rewrite (end of L55):**
> "Cantaloupe earns a cut of each card sale, plus monthly fees, without stocking a single snack. In May 2026, it was bought by a rival, 365 Retail Markets."

## Claim 6: Definitions

### 6a. Micro market: VERIFIED
"A micro market is an unlocked mini-store with open shelves, fresh food, and a self-checkout kiosk."
- Cantaloupe, "What are Micro Markets?", https://www.cantaloupe.com/what-are-micro-markets/ (undated): "Micro markets are unstaffed, self-service retail spaces that provide food and drink options in unexpected locations." "Micro markets often feature a mix of open shelving and refrigerated cases to offer sandwiches, salads, fruits, vegetables, and other perishable foods." "Instead of an employee at a register, micro markets have a self-checkout kiosk for customers to 'grab, pay, and go.'"
- FTC (May 1, 2026 release above): "Micromarkets are small unattended convenience stores often found in offices and breakrooms" that "provide access to affordable, freshly prepared food that is not available in vending machines."
The script's definition fits both. ("Unstaffed" would match Cantaloupe's wording more closely than "unlocked", but "unlocked" is a fair contrast with smart stores.)

### 6b. Smart stores: MOSTLY FAIR, optional fix
"smart stores, the AI-powered fridges you unlock with a card tap"
- Cantaloupe Smart Stores page, https://www.cantaloupe.com/solutions/point-of-sale/smart-stores/: customers "just tap their card to open the cabinet(s), grab their product(s), and go." "Proven weighted shelf technology and cameras track items removed from the store, with a 99% accuracy." "Both the 600 and 700 lines support cooler or ambient configurations."
- Cantaloupe's own Jan 9, 2024 release was titled "Cantaloupe Showcases AI-Powered Self-Service Retail Solutions at NRF '24", so "AI-powered" is Cantaloupe's own marketing term.
- The 2026 report (p. 11) describes Smart Stores as "fully cashless, frictionless" and able to carry "fresh meals to everyday essentials".
"Card tap to unlock" is exact. "Fridges" is not always right, because some units are unrefrigerated ("ambient") cabinets. And the tracking that does the work is weight sensors plus cameras.

**Optional rewrite (L59):** "At smart stores, the locked smart fridges and cabinets you open with a card tap, it was 4.49."

## Claim 7 (new): Industry consolidation, facts for the buyer audience

**Fact A: The two biggest micro market tech companies merged (2025–2026).**
365 Retail Markets (owned by private equity firm Providence Equity Partners) agreed on June 16, 2025 to buy Cantaloupe for $11.20 a share, about $848 million in equity value. It closed on May 11, 2026, after the FTC required it to sell Cantaloupe's Three Square Market kiosk business to Seaga. The FTC said the deal "would combine the two largest providers of micromarket kiosks." The order also requires 365 to give the FTC advance notice of any micromarket-kiosk acquisition for 10 years. Sources: SEC DEFA14A Ex. 99.1 (Jun 16, 2025); FTC press releases (May 1 and June 17, 2026); 365 closing release (May 11, 2026). URLs are under claim 5.

**Fact B: Canteen's network absorbed a bottler's entire vending business (April 2025).**
Effective April 7, 2025, Buffalo Rock, an Alabama Pepsi and Keurig Dr Pepper bottler, "divested its full-line vending operations to Five Star, Canteen North America, and other Canteen franchise operators across Alabama, West Georgia, and the Florida Panhandle." The operation had grown to "almost 700 micro markets and over 7,600 vending locations." Sources: Vending Connection, Apr 7, 2025, https://www.vendingconnection.com/?p=88563 ; VendingMarketWatch (Automatic Merchandiser), Apr 11, 2025, https://vendingmarketwatch.com/management/news/55282565/bottler-buffalo-rock-sells-vending-operations-to-canteen ; Business Alabama, https://businessalabama.com/buffalo-rock-sells-its-vending-operations-to-five-star-breaktime-solutions-and-canteen-north-america/ . These are trade press outlets quoting a company release. I could not find the original press release text.
Follow-on: Five Star Breaktime Solutions, "the largest franchise operator of Canteen, a division of Compass Group", has bought Jordan Distributing (TN), Krodel Vending (IN), AM Coffee LLC (LA) and West Chester Vending since the start of 2026. It later added Weber Custom Vending (OH). Source: Vending Times, May 21, 2026, https://www.vendingtimes.com/news/five-star-continues-acquisition-streak-with-west-chester-vending-buy

**Fact C: Aramark keeps buying regional operators.**
- July 15, 2024: Aramark Refreshments bought "substantially all of the assets" of Tomdra Inc. (more than 300 clients, southwest Arizona) and SunDun of Washington (more than 1,000 clients, Washington, D.C. area). Sources: Aramark newsroom, https://www.aramark.com/newsroom/news/2024/july/aramark-refreshments-expands-vending-and-micro-market-presence-w ; VendingMarketWatch, https://www.vendingmarketwatch.com/management/news/55126200/...
- Feb 24, 2026: Aramark Refreshments acquired "Legend Food Service LLC and Legend Commerce CT LLC", a vending and micro market operator from East Haven, Connecticut, serving "hundreds of clients throughout Connecticut and Rhode Island." Source: Aramark Refreshments, https://blog.aramarkrefreshments.com/blog/aramark-refreshments-expands-innovation-with-acquisition-of-legend-food-service-

**Not recommended:** IBISWorld's business counts. The Aug 2026 edition says "2,564 businesses", down 1.3% a year from 2021 to 2026. Earlier editions said 15,185 for 2025. The counts disagree by roughly six times across editions, so they are not usable on screen. NAMA's census publishes revenue, not a usable operator count, in the coverage I found. I could not query Census County Business Patterns, because the API now requires a key.

**Draft script passage (plain, calm style). Suggested placement: after L51 (scale) or L53 (Canteen).**
> "And the big players keep getting bigger. In 2025, the Alabama bottler Buffalo Rock sold its whole vending business, almost 700 micro markets and more than 7,600 vending locations, to Canteen and its franchise partners. A year later, the technology company 365 Retail Markets finished buying Cantaloupe for about 848 million dollars. Regulators only let it through after the company agreed to sell off a competing kiosk business."

Shorter alternative (two sentences):
> "The big players are buying up the small ones. Canteen's network took over an Alabama bottler's entire vending business in 2025, almost 7,600 locations, and in 2026 the two biggest micro market tech companies, 365 Retail Markets and Cantaloupe, merged in an 848-million-dollar deal."
