# Next topic pitch: owning an American motel

**Prepared:** Nov 27, 2026. **Builds on:** `research/competitor-landscape.md`, `docs/decision-report.md`, `research/ep1-packaging/`.
**Excludes:** vending machines (Episode 1) and car washes (Episode 2).

## The pitch in one paragraph

Make Episode 3 about **what a 60-room roadside motel actually keeps from a $67 room night**.
- **Demand:** motel ownership is producing outliers on six unrelated channels in the last 12 months, from 80× to 149× their channel norm, plus 1–2M-view AI fiction about "buying an abandoned motel."
- **Gap:** every video is either an owner vlog, the Patel immigration story, or a 1–2K-view "Economics of Owning a Motel" template. None shows the documents.
- **Documents:** the episode can be built on real filings. Red Roof's 2026 franchise disclosure gives 2025 room rate, occupancy and RevPAR for 508 inns. Wyndham's published fees, Blackstone's Motel 6 press releases ($1.9B in 2012, $525M in 2024) and SBA loan-level data all exist. California's Homekey records show what the state paid per motel room.
- **Fit for The Split:** a room night splits cleanly into booking site, franchisor, housekeeping and laundry, property tax and insurance, loan, and owner.
- **Fit for the viewer:** buying a motel is a classic purchase for this audience. Hotels and motels are the **#1 industry in SBA 7(a) lending by dollars** (our calculation below).

## 1. How the outliers were found

- **Data collected Nov 27, 2026 with yt-dlp:**
  - 1,057 channel Videos tabs, up to 200 recent uploads each.
  - 130 search queries, each run two ways: relevance, and "this year, sorted by views." That gave about 6,700 unique results.
- **Channels covered:**

  - Same-audience channels: Rob Berger, Ken McElroy, Money Guy, BiggerPockets and others.
  - Every channel that had a 50K+ video in the topic searches.
- **Outlier rule** (as before): views ÷ the median of the ~20 uploads around the video on the same channel ≥ 3×, **and** ≥ 3× the channel's recent median (last 30 long-form videos older than 3 weeks), **and** ≥ 50K views. Shorts are excluded.
  - Result: 9,555 outliers on 1,057 channels.
  - They were sorted into 36 business topics by title keywords, then checked by hand to drop games, kids' videos, crime news and non-US content.
- **Age is approximate.** YouTube only gives "N months/years ago" on channel pages, so "~1 yr" means 12–23 months. Watch pages are behind a bot check, so exact dates and like counts were not read.
- **Screenshots:** thumbnails are in two contact sheets sent in chat. They are kept out of the repo because they are other creators' images.

## 4. Our angle

**"Follow one $67 room night"**, with The Split as the spine and the Deal File as the proof:
1. **The room night.** Red Roof's FDD Item 19 gives the average franchised inn in 2025: ADR $67.08, occupancy 53.4%, RevPAR $35.79. Half the rooms sit empty on an average night.
2. **The Split of $67:**
   - booking site commission, on only the share booked that way;
   - franchisor royalty and marketing (Super 8: 5.5% + 3% of room revenue);
   - housekeeping, laundry and the front desk;
   - utilities;
   - property tax and insurance (insurance has been rising fastest);
   - the SBA loan payment;
   - what's left for the owner.
   - Build it from the documents below. Where only industry ranges exist (OTA commission, labor %), label them as ranges.
3. **The buyer's question:** what a 60-room motel sells for, and what the bank wants. Use SBA data: motels are SBA 7(a)'s biggest industry by dollars, with a 3.0% charge-off rate on resolved loans (FY2010–19).
4. **The twist:** Motel 6 itself. Blackstone paid $1.9B for 1,102 hotels in 2012 and sold the brand to OYO for $525M in 2024, after selling off most of the real estate. Explain why the brand ended up worth less than the buildings. That is the "who wins" beat, as in Episode 1.
5. **The exit door:** when the numbers stop working, owners sell to governments. California's Homekey bought 6,029 units (mostly motels) for $846M in 2020, about $140K per unit. Oregon's Turnkey paid about $85K per room.

**Tone:** calm and sourced, no immigrant stereotypes. AAHOA's own report is the source for who owns these motels (members own 59.5% of US hotels and **65.9% of economy hotels**), stated neutrally.

## 7. Risks

- **Sensitive ownership story.** It is easy to slip into stereotypes about Indian-American owners. Keep it to AAHOA's own figures and the economics.
- **One FDD, one brand.** Red Roof's numbers are the anchor. Say "a typical franchised Red Roof," not "every motel." Independents differ.
- **Costs are ranges, not filings.** The middle of The Split (labor, OTA, insurance) relies on paywalled or secondary data unless a real offering memorandum is found.
- **Overlap with car washes.** Both use the "private equity / big owner vs small owner" beat. Keep Motel 6's asset-light story as the twist and don't repeat Episode 2's roll-up framing.
- **Crowding.** About 12 tiny AI channels already use "The Economics of Owning a Motel." Avoid that title form entirely.
- **Footage.** Stock of real chain signs may show trademarks. Use generic roadside motels, or treat brand logos as editorial use only.
