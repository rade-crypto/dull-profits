## Things that contradict common assumptions
1. **Mister did not go private as a winner for shareholders.** Revenue went from $595M to $1.05B, sites from 344 to 548 and members from 1.4M to 2.3M. The buyout price was still $7, against a $15 IPO.
2. **Most of the biggest chain doesn't own its land.** 88.5% of Mister sites are leased. It owes $1.65B in future rent, more than its $800M term loan.
3. **Take 5 sold for about $1M per car wash**, after $1.18B of write-downs. Landlords pay $5–8M per site in sale-leasebacks. Owning the land was worth more than running the wash.
4. **Membership is not a moat.** Zips doubled members per site and still went bankrupt. Its interest bill rose by $34M in one year.
5. **The landlords mostly came out fine.** Getty's first tenant problem since 2011 was 1.8% of rent, and it expected to get back about 70% of it. EPRT stopped adding car washes in 2026, and they fell from its #1 to its #3 industry.
6. **Most big REITs don't own car washes at all.** Realty Income, Agree, FCPT and NETSTREIT never mention them in their 10-Ks.
7. **"Atlas" owned Zips is wrong.** It was Atlantic Street Capital. **"Roark owns Quick Quack" is wrong.** It is KKR plus Seidler. Roark was the seller of the old ICWG business to Driven.
8. **Turtle Wax Pro car wash chemicals are sold by Dover's OPW unit**, next to its PDQ and Belanger machines, so one industrial company sells the tunnel, the controls and the soap. Whether Dover owns the brand or licenses it was not checked.
