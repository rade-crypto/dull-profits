## 1. LAX vending deal

**Claim (lines 5, 43, 49):** "In 2023, [LAX] approved a vending deal where the operator pays 59.5 percent of gross sales, or 100 dollars per machine per month, whichever is more. The operator buys the stock and does the driving, and the airport collects for the floor space." Also: "one of the busiest airports in America."

**Verdict:** VERIFIED for the year, the percentage and the minimum. NEEDS FIX for "buys the stock and does the driving" (not stated in the source).

**Primary source:** LAWA, *Report to the Board of Airport Commissioners*, "Award of Vending Machine Concession RFB", Meeting Date 2/16/2023, item 2 (approver Dave Jones, Deputy Executive Director, Commercial Development).
- Staff report PDF: https://lawa.granicus.com/MetaViewer.php?view_id=4&clip_id=935&meta_id=59516
- Agenda, Feb 16, 2023: https://lawa.granicus.com/GeneratedAgendaViewer.php?view_id=4&clip_id=935

Key excerpts (verbatim):
> "Request to award a Non-Exclusive Vending Machine Services Concession Agreement to First Class Vending, Inc. for a five-year term, with two one-year options, generating a minimum of $86,400 in revenue the first year and $432,000 over the initial five-year term."

> "Pursuant to the terms of the proposed concession agreement, First Class will pay LAWA the greater of a minimum monthly guarantee of $100 per machine or 59.5 percent of gross sales over the five-year term and any approved options. First Class initially will operate 67 machines at LAX and five machines at the Van Nuys FlyAway building, both in employee only and public areas."

> "In addition, First Class is responsible for all associated maintenance of the machines."

> Bids received September 19, 2022 (% of gross sales): First Class Vending, Inc. 59.5%; "Compass Group USA, Inc. by and through its Canteen Division" 51.6%; Gilly Vending, Inc. 42.0%. "First Class was selected as the highest responsive bidder."

> "The vending machines are in public areas of LAX, including parking structures, non-secured public areas, post security checkpoints, and in the VNY FlyAway building."

**Confirmation and later update:** LAWA, *Report to the Board of Airport Commissioners*, Meeting Date 8/27/2026, item 21 (Second Amendment to LAA-9208).
- PDF: https://lawa.granicus.com/MetaViewer.php?view_id=4&clip_id=1270&meta_id=80139
> "02-16-2023 Resolution #27671 (LAA-9208) Award. Awarded a five-year Non-Exclusive Concession Agreement to Aramark Refreshment Services, LLC dba First Class Vending, Inc. ..."
> "Currently, First Class Vending pays LAWA 59.5 percent of gross sales for vending operations across LAX and VNY. The proposed amendment would implement a separate revenue structure for the micro market locations only, requiring the concessionaire to remit 20 percent of gross sales, less tax and California Redemption Value."

The award also went to the LA City Council (Council File 23-0641, documents dated June 14, 2023). Those City Clerk PDFs have moved to a sign-in-only system, so I could not open them. The Board's approval date, 2/16/2023, comes from LAWA's own reports.

**What this means for the script:**
- "2023" is correct: the Board of Airport Commissioners approved it on Feb 16, 2023 (Resolution 27671).
- 59.5% and the $100 minimum are correct. LAWA's August 2026 report says 59.5% still applies to vending. I could not confirm how the Board voted on the August 2026 amendment. The amendment only adds micro markets at LAWA staff facilities at 20%.
- The deal covers LAX **and** Van Nuys Airport (VNY): about 72 machines (67 at LAX, 5 at VNY).
- LAWA calls the operator "First Class Vending, Inc." (2023). The 2026 report calls it "Aramark Refreshment Services, LLC dba First Class Vending, Inc."
- The source does not say "buys the stock and does the driving". It does say "First Class is responsible for all associated maintenance". Reword.
- An optional detail: Compass Group's Canteen bid 51.6% and lost. That ties into the Canteen section.

**"One of the busiest airports in America": VERIFIED.** FAA, *Final CY2025 Enplanements at All Commercial Service Airports (by rank)*, Sept 24, 2026, ranks LAX 5th with 36,497,303 enplanements: https://www.faa.gov/airports/planning_capacity/passenger_allcargo_stats/passenger/ARP-CY2025-commercial-service-enplanements.pdf. FAA CY2024 (9/15/2025) also ranks it 5th, with 37,760,834.

**Suggested line 49:**
> "That airport is Los Angeles International. In 2023, its airport commission approved a vending deal where the operator pays 59.5 percent of gross sales, or 100 dollars per machine per month, whichever is more. The operator runs and maintains the machines. The airport just collects for the floor space."

Optional addition: "Even the runner-up bidder, Compass Group's Canteen, offered more than half."

## 4. The two-dollar soda model and the $900 month

**Claims (lines 27–41):** 6% sales tax, 10% commission, most customers paying by card. About 11¢ tax, about 94¢ product, about 9¢ card processing, about 4¢ reader and software, 20¢ location, about 62¢ left. "A machine selling 900 dollars a month... operator keeps about 197, roughly 22 percent margin."

**Source for the per-sale split:** VMFS USA, "Vending Machine Commission Rates: How Revenue Gets Split" (no byline or date; cites July 2026 data). https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/vending-machine-revenue-split-operator-vs-location-vs-supplier
> Assumptions: "prices include 6% sales tax, 78% of sales are cashless at about 5.95%, product cost is 50% of pre-tax sales," plus a $9.95 card reader plan and $7.99 for VMFS Cloud Basic.
> Image caption: "Illustrative donut chart splitting a $2.00 soda sale into supplier product cost, location commission, operator operating expenses and operator net profit".
> Table for a $1,000 month: sales tax $56.60; product cost $471.70; card processing $46.41; reader plan and software $17.94; location commission at 10% of gross $100.00; left over $307.35. "That last line is not profit."

The cent values in the donut chart are in an image I could not read. Scaling the $1,000 table to $2.00 reproduces the script's figures:

| Slice | Exact | Script |
|---|---|---|
| Sales tax (6%, included in price) | $0.1132 | ~11¢ ✓ |
| Product (50% of pre-tax $1.8868) | $0.9434 | ~94¢ ✓ |
| Card processing (78% × 5.95% × $2) | $0.0928 | ~9¢ ✓ |
| Reader and software (fixed monthly fee spread over a $1,000 month) | $0.0359 | ~4¢ ✓ |
| Location, 10% of **gross** | $0.2000 | 20¢ ✓ |
| Left for operator | **$0.6147** | "about 62¢" (61.5¢) |

- **The slices sum to $2.00?** Yes, with rounded slices: 11 + 94 + 9 + 4 + 20 + 62 = 200. The exact remainder is 61.5¢, so "about 61 cents" or "a little over 60 cents" is more precise. "About 62" is defensible as the rounded residual.
- **Commission on gross or pre-tax?** This supplier's default is **gross, including tax** ($100 on $1,000). The script's 20¢ matches that. On pre-tax sales the commission would be about 19¢ and the operator would keep about 62.6¢. Contracts differ: Las Positas and Riverside use "gross sales"; LAWA's 2026 micro-market terms say "gross sales, less tax and California Redemption Value."
- The reader and software cost is a flat monthly fee, so 4¢ per sale only holds at around $1,000 a month in sales. A slower machine pays more per can.

**Source for the $900 / $197:** the VMFS "How much can a vending machine really make" page (URL in section 3).
> At $900 gross with a 10% commission: net profit **$197, net margin 22%** (no commission: $287 / 32%; 5%: $242 / 27%; 20%: $107 / 12%).
> The example is a Slim Combo at a typical office (net $196.79, or 21.9%). Cost lines: product $432; commission $90; card processing $41.77; reader $9.95; VMFS Cloud $7.99; mileage $43.50 (60 mi at 72.5¢); insurance $40; shrink $18; maintenance reserve $20. The table labels the result "Before your own time."

**Verdict:** VERIFIED, with two clarifications. (a) The $197 is before the operator pays themselves for their time. (b) The supplier calls the site a "typical office", which the script calls "a decent location".

**Suggested lines:**
> Line 37: "That leaves the operator about 61 cents, before paying for gas, labor, repairs, insurance and income tax." (Optional; 62 is acceptable.)
> Line 41: "Run the full numbers on a machine selling 900 dollars a month at a typical office, and the same supplier estimates the operator keeps about 197. That's roughly a 22 percent margin, and it's before the operator pays themselves anything for their time."

## 5. Commissions, sales per machine, net per site and payback

**Source:** VMFS USA, "How much can a vending machine really make" (URL in section 3).

**5a. Commissions "commonly 5 to 20 percent": VERIFIED (as the supplier's statement).**
> "commonly somewhere between 5% and 20% of gross according to Nav and Gumball." FAQ: "Most locations that charge take 5% to 20% of gross sales."
> Corroboration: The Hustle (Zachary Crockett, dated June 24, 2024) gives a range of 5–25%. https://thehustle.co/we-interviewed-20-vending-machine-owners-heres-how-much-they-make

**5b. Sales per machine: VERIFIED (supplier estimate).**

| Tier | Gross/month | Net margin | Net/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak (<50 people/day) | under $150 | 0–15% | $0–20 |
| Typical (small office, shop, gym) | **$150–700** | 15–25% | **$20–175** |
| Strong (100+ daily users) | **$700–1,500** | 22–30% | $155–450 |
| Top tier (hospital, plant, transit hub) | **$2,000–3,000+** | 25–35% | $500–1,050+ |

Line 23 states these ranges as fact without attribution. They are one equipment seller's estimates, so attribute them. Independent check: The Hustle's survey of 23 operators found an average of $309 gross per machine per month, with a range of $75 to $650.

**Suggested line 23:**
> "Sales per machine depend almost entirely on where it stands. One supplier's guide puts a typical site at 150 to 700 dollars a month. A strong one does up to 1,500. Top spots like hospitals, factories and transit hubs can do 2,000 or more."

**5c. "A typical site nets about 20 to 175 dollars a month": VERIFIED (same table; "net" here includes mileage and upkeep but not the owner's labor).**

**Payback math:** NEEDS FIX.
- $3,500 / $175 = **20 months** (1.67 years). "A year and a half" understates it.
- $3,500 / $20 = 175 months = **14.6 years**. "More than 14 years" ✓.
- Using the supplier's actual combo price of $3,450, the range is 19.7 months to 14.4 years.
- The supplier's own payback example counts the **all-in** startup cost, not just the machine: Slim Combo at a typical office with a 10% commission takes "27 months" on about $5,300 total ($3,650 machine + $450 reader + $400 shipping + $500 opening inventory + $300 setup; $5,300 / $197 ≈ 27). The real payback is longer than machine-price math suggests.

**Suggested line 71:**
> "Now do the math on an ordinary location. One supplier estimates a typical site nets about 20 to 175 dollars a month. At that rate, a 3,500-dollar combo machine takes anywhere from about 20 months to more than 14 years just to pay for the machine. And that's if nothing breaks."

**A tension to know about (not in the script):** the same supplier's product page for its Smart Combo machine (https://vmfsusa.com/product/classic-combo-vending-machine) says: "In a typical office, 210 people pass by each day, 6 in 100 buy at $3. That is about $2,268 in monthly sales. You keep estimated $938." and "it pays for itself in about 6 months." That is far above the same company's blog figure of $150–700 for a typical site. Do not name the company on air when contrasting the two. "Even suppliers' own sales pages are far rosier than their own guides" is fair if you keep the receipts.

## 8. FTC "Operation Vend Up Broke"

**Claim (line 77):** "In 1998, the Federal Trade Commission and ten states announced Operation Vend Up Broke. It was a sweep of 40 enforcement actions against companies selling vending business opportunities... Some sellers even paid fake references to vouch for the profits."

**Verdict:** VERIFIED. Tighten the last sentence to match the FTC's wording.

**Source:** FTC press release, "FTC Announces 'OPERATION VEND UP BROKE': States and Feds Stop Business Opportunity Scams", **September 3, 1998**: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/1998/09/ftc-announces-operation-vend-broke-states-and-feds-stop-business
> "In an ongoing campaign to combat vending machine scams, law enforcement officials in 10 states" joined the FTC in announcing a sweep of **40 enforcement actions** (4 by the FTC, 36 by the states) against fraudulent vending business opportunities.
> Jodie Bernstein, Director of the FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection: "Every year, thousands of consumers literally end up broke after investing in purportedly lucrative vending opportunities." She warned: "You can't rely on supposedly satisfied consumers supplied by the seller." Such references are often "singers" paid to give favorable reports.
> In the Vendors Financial Services case, the FTC alleged the company supplied "phony references" to back its earnings claims.

**Suggested line 77 ending:**
> "...The pitch back then was easy routes and big money. The FTC warned that some sellers paid fake references, people it called 'singers', to vouch for the profits."

(These were allegations in the FTC's cases. "The FTC warned" or "according to the FTC" keeps the attribution right.)

## 9. NEW: What do established routes sell for?

**Sources:**

A. **BizBuySell valuation benchmarks, vending machine businesses.** https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/vending-machine-business/
BizBuySell returns HTTP 403 to automated tools, so the figures below come from search-engine extraction of that page, not a direct read. **Open the page in a browser and confirm them before publishing.**
- "Sale price multiples based on vending machine businesses sold on BizBuySell during five years between 2021 and 2025."
- Earnings (SDE) multiple of sold businesses: lower quartile 1.80, **median 2.15**, upper quartile 2.62 (average 2.35).
- Revenue multiple: median 1.11 (quartiles 0.88–1.33).
- **Median sale price $83,500** against a median asking price of $88,498. Median revenue $71,000; median owner earnings $39,601.
- A vending business "consistently generating sales over $130k annually may sell for an earnings multiple above 2.5," while a route under $50k in sales would sell "closer to 1.8."
- Note: "sold" figures on BizBuySell are reported by brokers and sellers, not audited transactions.

B. **Independent snapshot of current BizBuySell listings.** VendBuddy, "Vending Routes for Sale: Prices and Multiples (2026)", snapshot dated Oct 1, 2026: https://vendbuddy.io/blog/vending-routes-for-sale
- 234 established US listings. Median asking price **$120,000** (middle half $48,000–$168,000).
- Median ask **2.3× annual cash flow** (middle half 1.4×–3.4×) across about 130 listings that disclosed cash flow. About 100 of 237 established listings showed no cash-flow figure at all.
- Median about $5,700 per machine (28 listings stating a machine count).
- "Asking prices are what sellers want, not what routes sold for." Listed cash flow is SDE, "before the owner pays themselves."

C. **A concrete listing.** The Route Exchange (route broker), "Chicago Area Vending Route for Sale – 12 Machines Within a 15-Mile Service Area" (Route 800), no posting date (©2026; viewed Nov 26, 2026): https://therouteexchange.com/chicago-area-vending-route-for-sale-12-machines-within-a-15-mile-service-area/
- **Asking $138,000.** 12 machines (11 placed, 1 coffee machine in storage) at 7 locations in Chicago, Oak Park and Glenview.
- Average weekly sales about $2,030 (about **$105,560 a year**). Seller-estimated net about $945 a week (**$49,241 a year**). About 8–12 hours of work a week.
- That works out to about **2.8×** the seller's net estimate and 1.3× sales.
- "Financial figures are based on seller-provided information." P&Ls are available for review. No vehicle is included.
