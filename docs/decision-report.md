# Dull Profits · Decision report

*November 26, 2026. This covers what was researched, who the competitors are, what their audiences say, our differentiator, and why each decision was made.*

---

## 1. The short version

- **Our lane is open.** No channel, faceless or on camera, makes calm, sourced videos about the numbers of one small business at a time.
  - The big faceless explainers (Modern MBA, Wendover) cover national brands.
  - The "boring business" space is split between two kinds of channel:
    - On-camera owners counting cash ("I bought a car wash and it made me $500,000").
    - AI channels mass-producing "10 boring businesses" lists.
- **People want these topics.** Car-wash videos hit 3.7M views, laundromats 4.9M, vending 10.5M. None of those top videos is a calm "follow the money" explainer.
- **The audience's biggest complaint is missing numbers, not tone.** The most-liked criticisms say creators leave out the owner's own wage, taxes, repairs and loan guarantees. They also say "it's not passive" and that the course is the real product.
- **Our differentiator:** *the only channel that shows the document behind every number.* It's calm and faceless, takes one business at a time, and shows it from the side of the person who would buy, own or sell it. (Section 5.)
- **Episode 1 stays as voiced.** The changes are on screen only, so **you don't need to regenerate any voiceover.** (Section 7.)
- **What I need from you:** an OK on the differentiator and the Episode 1 changes, plus a title pick. After that, in order: lock the style, top up the B-roll, make the stills storyboard, then make the video.

**What I couldn't reach:**
- **YouTube comments.** YouTube blocks automated access from this machine with a bot check. I didn't try to get around it. Instead, the audience research used the places this audience talks:
  - BiggerPockets forums (real-estate investors with capital)
  - Car Wash Forum (working operators, some with 30+ years)
  - Goodreads reviews of the competitors' books
  - Hacker News
  - Trustpilot reviews of vending "done-for-you" companies
  - six trust surveys on AI content
- **Reddit** is blocked from this machine. Reddit quotes appear only where news articles quoted them.
- **The competitors' narration** couldn't be played, so "faceless / AI" labels come from thumbnails and upload pace.

**Definitions used below:**
- **Median:** the median views of a channel's last 30 long videos.
- **Views/sub:** median views ÷ subscribers, i.e. how much of the audience actually watches.
- **Outlier:** a video with at least 3× the views of the channel's surrounding uploads.

## 4. What their audiences actually say

| Theme | How often | Strongest signal | Representative quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"It's not passive; you're buying a job"** | ~26 of 110 | Laundromat seller's write-up, 43 likes | "Dont judge this business off a you tubers videos." (car-wash operator, 13 reactions) |
| **"The numbers leave things out"**: owner's wage, taxes, repairs, cash counted as revenue | ~17 | Top *Buy Then Build* review, **45 likes**: attacks counting the owner's own wage as return and skipping taxes and SBA personal guarantees | "3 years tax returns are the only numbers you should trust. Everything else can be cooked." (11 reactions) |
| **"The guru is selling the course"** | ~11 | Top *Main Street Millionaire* review, **44 likes**: why does someone with "over 23 businesses" sell a $2k course? | — |
| **Location, lease and real estate are the real asset** | ~11 | HN "the real business of car washes is real estate" (17 replies) | "A Laundromat without a lease is a truck load of used equipment." |
| **Scams: locators, done-for-you packages** | ~12 | VendPros: 5 of 6 Trustpilot reviews are 1-star ($30k and $50k losses) | "He provided me with 'leads', not actual locations." |
| **AI voice as the "slop" giveaway** | ~13 | HN "AI Slop Report" thread (159 points); Raptive: suspected-AI content loses ~half its trust | "I don't mind AI voice overs. I am appalled by the fact that people… don't bother to listen to it to remove the awkward pauses and blatant mispronunciations." |
| **Cash piles and clickbait** | ~9 | Operators mock bill-changer cash shown as "revenue" | "make a big show of taking cash out of their bill changers and brag about the 'revenue'" |
| **What gets praised** | ~8 | Honesty about costs and failures, even when it talks the viewer out of buying | "He talks about the various costs and problems." |

**What the 40+ owner/buyer crowd says specifically:**
- They're already skeptical of YouTube. One buyer wrote: "I am 53… tons of YouTube videos… not all content is legitimate."
- They think in real-estate terms.
- Their checklist is always the same:
  - the lease
  - 3 years of tax returns
  - equipment age
  - card vs cash revenue
  - insurance
  - who covers the shifts
- **The seller side is almost unserved:** "Why is so much attention paid to the buyer… and none to the folks who sold?"

**Questions nobody answers:**
- What do I actually take home after paying myself, the loan and repairs?
- What's it worth, and which multiple applies?
- How do I verify cash revenue?
- Can I get an SBA loan for $100–500k?
- What happens when the lease ends?
- If I'm selling at 60, who buys, and does private equity pay more?

**About AI voices specifically:**
- In blind tests, good AI voices score close to human ones.
- The penalty comes from the things viewers name:
  - mispronunciations and odd pauses
  - padded scripts
  - footage that doesn't match the words
  - epic music
  - long intros
- Older viewers get fed the most "slop", and their adult children notice.

## 5. Our differentiator

> **Dull Profits is the only channel that shows the document behind every number.**
> It's calm and faceless, takes one business at a time, and shows it from the side of the person who would buy, own or sell it.

How each part answers what the research found:

| Pillar | What it is | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Proof, not claims: the Deal File** | Every key number appears with its real source document (contract, filing, report), highlighted | Owners trust documents ("tax returns are the only numbers you should trust"). On-camera owners prove numbers by showing their books; we can't, so we show the public record. **No competitor uses this look** |
| **2. The Split plus "what you actually keep"** | One real transaction sliced into who gets paid, ending with the line nobody shows: after your own wage, the loan and repairs | The two most-liked criticisms (45 and 44 likes) are about exactly these missing numbers |
| **3. The owner's checklist** | Every episode ends with the same due-diligence card: lease · tax returns · equipment age · card vs cash · who covers shifts | It's the checklist experienced owners repeat; it becomes a series signature |
| **4. Buy, own, *and sell*** | The seller's side gets real attention (valuation, multiples, what private equity pays) | The largest content gap; our 50–65 viewers are often the sellers |
| **5. Calm and TV-readable, the anti-hype** | No cash piles, no "SHOCKED", no course, no epic music; big type, slow cuts | Patrick Boyle / Rob Berger tone wins with this audience; cash piles and courses are the top distrust signals |
| **6. Quality over volume** | 2–4 episodes a month, unique research and graphics each time | Mass producers sit at 1K–18K medians and match YouTube's "inauthentic content" pattern |

**Description footer every episode:** "Narration uses a synthetic voice. Researched and written by Dull Profits. No course, no coaching, no affiliate links. Not financial advice."

## 7. What changes in Episode 1

**All of these are on screen, so the voiceover stays as is.**

1. **Title.** Pick one; the other two become YouTube "Test & Compare" variants.
   - **A.** "A Vending Machine Sells $900 a Month. The Owner Keeps $197." *(recommended: real $ figures, a statement, a skeptical twist)*
   - **B.** "LAX Takes 59.5% of Every Vending Sale. Here's Who Gets the Rest."
   - **C.** "Who Actually Gets Your $2 at a Vending Machine"
2. **"What you actually keep."** The $900 → $197 graphic gets a footnote line: "before your own time, the loan and income tax". On the route-multiple graphic (9:11), a note says the earnings include the owner's own wage.
3. **Today's version of Vend Up Broke.** During "the ads have moved from newspapers to TikTok", a Deal File card shows a real 1-star review: "He provided me with 'leads', not actual locations." (Trustpilot, vending locator).
4. **The owner's checklist** closes the episode at "ask which of those three it actually owns": lease and location contracts · machine-level sales records · equipment age · card vs cash · who restocks when you can't.
5. **No cash-pile shots.** "Let the cash pile up" shows the quote card being struck through, not money.
6. **Stand-in footage dropped:** grocery-shelf "restocking" and generic "repair" clips. Those lines become our route-map graphic and a "service visit" checklist graphic.
7. **Thumbnail:** The Split bar with the cream slice labelled "OWNER 62¢" and a small highlighted document corner (the Deal File look nobody else uses).
8. **Description:** the footer from section 5, plus chapters and every source link (already listed in `fact-check.md`).
