# What the two experts advised

Full versions: `docs/editing-playbook.md` (documentary editor) and `docs/motion-playbook.md` (motion designer).

## Documentary editor

1. **Footage first.** 55–65% of the video is sharp footage. Graphics on their own background take at most 25%, and documents 10–15%.
2. **Short shots.** Footage shots run 2.5–6 s and never more than 8 s. Something new appears on screen at least every 4 s.
3. **Nothing ever looks stuck.**
   - Footage always plays: no frozen frames and no slowed-down holds.
   - A graphic's background is real footage, softened and darkened, slowly pushing in, with a new clip about every 6 s.
4. **Show exactly what the voice says.** When the voice says "restocking", show hands restocking a machine, not a man on his phone or a skyline.
5. **Numbers.**
   - Each number counts up as it's spoken, never before.
   - Units are written out: "$7 billion", and "More than" instead of "+".
   - The on-screen number matches the voice exactly ($3,500, not $3,450), and each one shows its source.
6. **The sandwich.** A long graphic cuts to footage for 2–3 s, then comes back exactly as it left.
7. **Transitions.**
   - Hard cuts by default.
   - A focus pull into a graphic when the background is the same clip.
   - A dissolve only for a jump in time or archive film.
8. **Placement.**
   - No text over faces.
   - Nothing in the bottom 15% of the frame.
   - No dark overlays or boxes behind text.
   - No presenter lines.
9. **Your five comment spots:**
   - **0:26, the pitch:** drop the strike-through. The quotes stack up over a stocked machine, then the cash.
   - **1:00, $7 billion:** the graphic starts when the sentence starts, the number counts up, and the van aerial runs behind it.
   - **1:05, the company names:** food-service footage with Compass and Aramark, then a US map filling with small operators.
   - **1:13, the route:** the map zooms into a moving aerial. The pins and route stick to the houses, then cut to restocking, cash and repair clips.
   - **1:22, machine prices:** line drawings of each machine draw themselves, and each price counts up as it's said.
10. **New clips.** Download about 30 more clips that literally show what's being said.

## Motion designer

1. **Why it looked stuck.** Measured on Episode 1: the graphics stood still for 76% of their screen time.
2. **Three things keep moving:**
   - the footage always plays;
   - a slow camera drift and push runs on every graphic;
   - something new, tied to a spoken word, happens at least every 2.5 s.
3. **Stronger entrances and real exits.**
   - Numbers rise and sharpen into place.
   - Bars grow with the value riding the tip.
   - Every graphic leaves in 0.45 s instead of vanishing at the cut.
4. **The background and the route.**
   - A real lens focus pull replaces the cross-fade, which looked ghosted.
   - The route line is thicker, with a moving marker driving it, so it reads on a TV.
5. **Following the voice.**
   - The item being talked about is highlighted. Other items are never greyed out.
   - Quotes appear word by word as they're read.
   - Documents land like paper.
6. **Specific fixes.**
   - The Split recap gets a beat for each slice as it's named.
   - The Split doesn't restart across a cut.
   - Every graphic is checked for still stretches before rendering.
